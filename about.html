Theory & Principles

About NMR Well Logging

A concise guide to the physics, equations, and interpretation behind NMR log analysis.

What is NMR?

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is a technique used to analyze fluids within porous rocks by measuring the response of hydrogen nuclei in a magnetic field.

Since hydrogen is present in both oil and water, NMR provides direct information about fluid distribution inside the reservoir.

Working Principle

When a magnetic field is applied, hydrogen nuclei align with the field.

  • A radio frequency (RF) pulse is applied, causing the nuclei to become excited.
  • When the pulse is removed, the nuclei return to equilibrium and emit signals.
  • These signals are analyzed to determine fluid properties.

T₂ Relaxation & Pore Size

The T₂ relaxation time represents how quickly the signal decays. It is strongly related to pore size:

Short T₂ → Small pores → Bound Fluids
Long T₂ → Large pores → Movable Fluids

This relationship allows NMR to characterize pore structure.

T₂ Distribution

The NMR measurement produces a T₂ distribution curve. Each point on the curve has an amplitude Aᵢ, which represents the volume of fluid in a specific pore size range.

The total area under the curve represents the total fluid content in the rock.

Porosity Equation

Porosity is computed directly from the NMR signal amplitudes:

φ = C · Σ Aᵢ
φ = Porosity
Aᵢ = Signal amplitude
C = Calibration constant

Porosity is directly proportional to the NMR signal.

Bound vs Free Fluid

A cutoff time (typically 33 ms) is used to separate fluids:

T₂ < Tcutoff
Bound Fluid Volume (BFV)
Trapped in small pores — immovable
T₂ > Tcutoff
Free Fluid Index (FFI)
In large pores — can flow

FFI & BFV Equations

The total porosity is partitioned into movable and immobile fluid components:

FFI = φ × ( Σ Aᵢ [T₂ > Tcutoff] / Σ Aᵢ )
BFV = φ × ( Σ Aᵢ [T₂ < Tcutoff] / Σ Aᵢ )

Permeability — SDR Model

Permeability is estimated using the Schlumberger-Doll Research (SDR) model:

k = 4 · φ⁴ · T₂LM²
k = Permeability (mD)
T₂LM = Logarithmic mean of T₂
φ = Porosity

Higher T₂ and porosity values indicate better permeability.

Interpretation of Results

NMR results help evaluate reservoir quality:

↑
High FFI — good production potential
↑
High BFV — more trapped fluids
↑
Long T₂ — larger pores, better flow
↑
High k — higher permeability

These parameters guide reservoir engineering decisions.

💡
Key Insight

NMR directly measures fluids in the pore space, making it one of the most reliable tools for reservoir evaluation.
